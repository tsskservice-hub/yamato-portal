How to Play & Rules

How the game flows

  • The game uses all 48 hanafuda cards (12 months × 4 cards). Each player starts with 8 cards in hand and 8 cards on the field, and turns alternate starting with the dealer.
  • On your turn, first play one card from your hand. If a field card shares its month, you capture both; otherwise your card stays on the field. Then flip the top card of the deck and match it the same way.
  • If two field cards share the played card's month, you choose which one to take. If three are there, you take all four (a sweep).
  • When your captured cards complete a yaku (scoring combination), choose “Finish” to lock in the points or “Koi-Koi” to keep playing for more.
  • If both hands run out with no yaku completed, the month is a draw (nagare). The dealer alternates each month.

Koi-Koi and doubling

  • If a player who called “Koi-Koi” finishes next, their score doubles. If the opponent finishes first instead, the opponent's score doubles.
  • A finish whose yaku total 7 points or more also doubles the score. All doublings stack (for example, 7+ points with both players having called Koi-Koi is ×8).
  • If a player is dealt all four cards of one month, they win the month immediately for 6 points (a hand yaku). If all four cards of a month appear on the field, the month is redealt.

Card types

  • Brights (tagged “Bright”): the Crane (Pine), Curtain (Cherry Blossom), Moon (Susuki Grass), Rain (Willow), and Phoenix (Paulownia) — 5 cards.
  • Animals (tagged “Animal”): 10 cards, including birds, the butterfly, boar, deer, and the Sake Cup. The Chrysanthemum Sake Cup also counts as a Chaff card.
  • Ribbons (tagged “Ribbon”): 10 cards. The ribbons of January–March (Pine, Plum Blossom, Cherry Blossom) form the Red Ribbons; those of June, September, and October (Peony, Chrysanthemum, Maple) form the Blue Ribbons.
  • Chaff (tagged “Plain”): the remaining plain cards. Paulownia has three Chaff cards.
